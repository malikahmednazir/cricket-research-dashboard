07 / RESEARCH FINDINGS

What the data can tell us

The tables rank the players and teams represented in the provided data. Rankings are descriptive summaries, not proof that one player or team is universally better. Compare performance within a consistent format, season, role and sample size before drawing conclusions.

No statistical conclusion is hard-coded: visible rankings are computed from the CSV summaries at runtime.

08 / METHODOLOGY & LIMITATIONS

How to read this dashboard

  • Source file: supplied t20_bbb.csv, aggregated into compact CSV summaries for web deployment.
  • Batting runs, balls and boundaries are aggregated from delivery-level records.
  • Bowling economy uses legal balls divided by six; wides are excluded from the legal-ball count.
  • Dataset coverage and dismissal/result edge cases may affect derived values.
  • Demonstration research interface; not live, and not a verified ball-by-ball prediction model.
09 / RESEARCH PAPER CONNECTION

Dynamic cricket match outcome prediction

Goel et al. (2021) study ball-by-ball IPL outcome prediction, using historical seasons 2008–2018 for training and 2019 matches for testing. Their approach updates match context during the second innings and considers features such as current score, target, remaining balls and wickets. This dashboard currently visualises descriptive dataset summaries; it does not reproduce their trained prediction model.

Goel, R. et al. (2021). “Dynamic cricket match outcome prediction.” Journal of Sports Analytics, 7, 185–196. DOI: 10.3233/JSA-200510.
